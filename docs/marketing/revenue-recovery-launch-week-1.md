**Missed call → two-way response → qualified opportunity → booked estimate → verified job**

Why keep the stages separate?

- A returned message does not prove the caller is qualified.
- A qualified caller does not yet have an appointment.
- A booked estimate is not a signed job.
- Estimated pipeline is not collected revenue.

## Day 5 — LinkedIn Founder Post

**Post**

One calculation mistake can dramatically inflate the apparent value of missed roofing calls:

Using an **estimate-to-close rate** as though it were a **lead-to-signed-job rate**.

Those are not the same.

If only part of your legitimate inbound leads reach an estimate, the fuller calculation is:

**Missed calls × legitimate opportunity rate × estimate-booking rate × estimate-to-close rate × average job value**

For example, a roofing company should not assume every legitimate caller reaches the estimate stage. Contact, qualification, territory, capacity and scheduling all sit between the first call and the sales appointment.

Good revenue-recovery reporting should make those drop-off points visible—not hide them inside an inflated projection.

## Day 6 — LinkedIn Company Post

**Post**

Roofing demand does not follow office hours.

A homeowner may notice a leak after work. A storm may arrive at night. Your office may be closed while crews are still in the field.

Before adding after-hours coverage, define these operating rules:

- Business hours and forwarding triggers
- Service area and accepted job types
- New-lead qualification questions
- Existing-customer routing
- Appointment availability and capacity
- Urgent-call and safety wording
- Staff notification and human-escalation rules

The goal is to acknowledge the caller, collect useful information and establish an approved next step—without promising emergency dispatch, an arrival time or service availability that has not been confirmed.

## Day 6 — YouTube Short 2

**Title:** What Should Happen After an After-Hours Roofing Call?

**On-screen hook:** Your office is closed. The homeowner still needs an answer.

**Script (approximately 40 seconds)**

A homeowner finds an active leak at eight o’clock at night and calls your roofing company.

If nobody answers, a responsible after-hours workflow should do four things:

First, acknowledge the inquiry.

Second, identify whether this is a new opportunity or an existing customer.

Third, collect the property, roofing need, service-area fit and reported urgency.

Fourth, offer an approved appointment, callback or escalation path.

It should never diagnose the roof or promise that a crew is on the way unless your company has confirmed it.

That is revenue recovery with clear operational boundaries.
