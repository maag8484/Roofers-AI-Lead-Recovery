# Roof AI: First 10 Customers Activation and Retention Playbook

Prepared: September 4, 2026  
Status: Internal operating playbook. This document does not authorize customer contact, pricing changes, trial changes, refunds, credits, guarantees, or publication.

## Objective

Convert qualified 7-day trials into durable $299/month customers by getting each roofing company technically live, proving that its approved call workflow works, and making customer value visible without claiming that every missed call is a lead or guaranteeing appointments, jobs, or revenue.

For the first ten customers, use a concierge process rather than treating onboarding as a self-serve software event. The primary goal is **reliable activation**; the secondary goal is **documented outcome value**. Acquisition is not complete until the account is configured, tested, live, and understood by the customer.

## First-10 operating principles

1. **One accountable onboarding owner.** One Roof AI owner maintains the checklist, coordinates required inputs, records decisions, and closes open items.
2. **Configure before promoting.** Do not send real calls into an untested workflow.
3. **Separate controllable activation from market outcomes.** Roof AI can control configuration, testing, reporting, and support. It cannot control incoming call volume, caller quality, the roofer's capacity, close rate, or collected revenue.
4. **Use the customer's real operating rules.** Service area, job types, business hours, escalation, appointment capacity, and existing-customer routing must be explicitly approved.
5. **Make value auditable.** Preserve the original source and record each stage separately: eligible missed call, response, two-way conversation, qualified opportunity, approved handoff, confirmed estimate, signed job, and collected revenue.
6. **Escalate ambiguity.** Do not invent a service promise, diagnose a roof, interpret insurance coverage, or imply emergency dispatch.
7. **Learn from ten, not just one.** Record friction and changes in a common log so fixes improve the next account.

## Customer fit before trial start

A qualified first-10 trial should meet all of the following:

- Operates a legitimate U.S. roofing business with an identifiable owner or operator.
- Already receives inbound phone calls; Roof AI is not being purchased as a source of new leads.
- Has a phone system or carrier that can support the intended forwarding route.
- Can name its service territory and accepted roofing job types.
- Has a clear staff owner for estimates, existing customers, and escalations.
- Has usable appointment availability or agrees to a request-and-confirm workflow.
- Can complete required setup inputs within 24 hours of trial start.
- Understands that a missed call is not automatically a roofing opportunity or lost revenue.

Defer the trial when the company has no inbound volume, no capacity to serve more work, cannot provide routing rules, cannot maintain its calendar, expects guaranteed jobs or revenue, or needs unsupported emergency dispatch.

## Activation and first-value definitions

### Activation

An account is **activated** only when all six conditions are true:

1. Core company profile and accountable customer contact are complete.
2. Business hours, service area, job types, capacity, and caller routes are approved.
3. Phone forwarding or missed-call trigger is connected.
4. Appointment or callback destination and staff notifications are connected.
5. Required test scenarios pass and evidence is recorded.
6. The customer receives and acknowledges a one-page go-live summary.

Creating an account, entering a credit card, or importing a phone number is not activation.

### First value

Use two levels so retention does not depend on a random number of real calls during a short trial.

**Operational first value — required during the trial:** A realistic missed-call test is detected, follows the customer-approved path, captures the expected details, reaches the correct notification or calendar destination, and appears in the outcome record. This is the first-value milestone Roof AI can directly influence.

**Market first value — measured after go-live, never guaranteed:** A real eligible missed caller receives a response and reaches one of these verified outcomes:

- two-way homeowner conversation;
- qualified roofing opportunity;
- approved callback or human handoff;
- confirmed estimate; or
- existing-customer request correctly routed.

Report the earliest verified outcome and continue advancing the record. Never convert estimated pipeline into claimed revenue. A signed job and collected revenue require confirmation from the roofing company.

## Seven-day trial milestones

The trial clock and billing terms remain the currently published terms. Any change to when the trial begins, extension, credit, or refund requires separate approval.

| Timing | Customer milestone | Roof AI action | Exit evidence | At-risk trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-trial | Fit and readiness confirmed | Record inbound-call need, phone system, capacity, decision-maker, and required integrations | Readiness checklist complete | Critical technical prerequisite or operating owner missing |
| Day 0 | Trial handoff accepted | Create one onboarding record; send/prepare the approved input checklist; book or define the setup checkpoint | Owner, contact method, target go-live date | No accountable contact or no setup time reserved |
| Day 1 | Business rules complete | Validate company identity, hours, service area, job types, new-versus-existing routes, urgent-call wording, and prohibited promises | Configuration brief approved | Less than 80% of required inputs complete by end of day |
| Day 2 | Connections complete | Configure forwarding/trigger, notifications, calendar or request-and-confirm path, caller ID, and fallback | Connection checklist with timestamps | Forwarding, calendar, or notification destination not working |
| Day 3 | Tests pass | Run routine new lead, existing customer, out-of-area, after-hours, urgent-language, human-request, and failed-transfer tests | Test log; no unresolved critical defect | Any critical path fails or output reaches wrong person |
| Day 4 | Controlled go-live | Confirm approved rules, fallback, monitoring owner, capacity, and rollback path; enable only the agreed coverage | Go-live record and customer summary | Customer has not acknowledged routing or capacity |
| Day 5 | Live-flow review | Review eligible events, classifications, response delivery, exceptions, and false positives; fix Roof AI-controlled configuration defects | Daily health note and change log | No monitoring evidence, repeated misclassification, or delivery issue |
| Day 6 | Value and fit review | Present operational first-value evidence; report real outcomes only if they occurred; confirm staff follow-up ownership | First-value record and open-actions list | Operational first value not demonstrated or staff handoffs untouched |
| Day 7 | Paid-readiness review | Confirm system health, next 30-day objectives, reporting cadence, support path, and billing expectations | Paid-readiness decision with reason | Unresolved critical defect, misunderstood promise, or no operating owner |

### Trial completion statuses

- **Activated and paid-ready:** all activation requirements passed; customer understands the service and support path.
- **Activated but outcome pending:** operational first value passed, but insufficient live call volume produced a market outcome. Do not label this failure or invent value.
- **Blocked by customer prerequisite:** required access, phone change, calendar, rules, or owner remains missing. Record exact dependency and do not obscure it as a product defect.
- **Blocked by Roof AI:** a Roof AI-controlled configuration, integration, or support failure prevents activation. Escalate immediately and apply only an approved remediation.
- **Poor fit:** the customer's needs, volume, expectations, territory, capacity, or emergency requirements do not match the service. Recommend a clean stop rather than forcing conversion.

## Guided onboarding checklist

### 1. Business and owner

- Legal/trading company name and primary operating location
- Onboarding owner and backup contact
- Business hours, time zone, holidays, and storm-mode hours
- Published phone number and phone carrier/system
- Desired coverage: missed, busy, overflow, after-hours, or approved combination

### 2. Qualification and routing

- Service cities, counties, or ZIP codes
- Residential, commercial, repair, replacement, inspection, storm, and other accepted work
- Explicit exclusions and minimum-job rules
- New prospect, existing customer, vendor, applicant, solicitation, spam, and out-of-area routes
- Urgent-call facts to collect and approved safety/escalation language
- Human-transfer triggers, destination, timeout, and failed-transfer fallback
- Statements the system may never make

### 3. Appointment and capacity

- Direct-book versus request-and-confirm by appointment type
- Calendar owner, appointment durations, travel buffers, notice limits, and blackout periods
- Daily/weekly capacity and storm-mode restrictions
- Confirmation, reschedule, cancellation, and no-show process
- Owner for unconfirmed requests

### 4. Technical and reporting

- Forwarding condition and ring delay
- Caller ID, voicemail, call return, and service-unavailable behavior
- SMS/voice identity, consent, disclosure, opt-out, recording, and retention configuration as applicable
- Staff notification destinations and backup
- Required CRM/calendar fields and minimum necessary data
- Original acquisition source versus recovery channel
- Reporting owner and weekly review time

### 5. Test suite

Every scenario must show expected detection, response, classification, routing, notification, record, and fallback:

1. New in-area repair or replacement prospect
2. Existing customer with an active project
3. Out-of-service-area caller
4. After-hours inquiry
5. Reported active leak or safety-sensitive wording
6. Caller requests a person
7. Calendar unavailable or at capacity
8. Transfer recipient does not answer
9. Caller opts out or should not be contacted
10. Duplicate call attempt tied to the same need

Defects are classified as critical, major, or minor. A critical defect includes a wrong recipient, unsafe promise, failed opt-out, false confirmed appointment, missing fallback, or loss of required caller context. No controlled go-live occurs with an open critical defect.

## Support cadence for the first ten

This is the proposed operating cadence; customer-facing commitments require approval before use.

| Moment | Touchpoint | Purpose | Internal output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-trial/Day 0 | Readiness and kickoff checkpoint | Confirm fit, inputs, owner, and go-live target | Readiness record |
| Days 1–3 | Daily asynchronous setup check | Close dependencies and surface defects early | Red/amber/green health note |
| Day 3 | Test review | Confirm scenarios and corrections | Signed-off test log |
| Day 4 | Go-live confirmation | Restate active routes, fallback, and capacity | One-page go-live summary |
| Days 5–7 | Daily exception review | Verify live behavior and staff handoffs | Exception/change log |
| Day 6 or 7 | Value and paid-readiness review | Show operational proof, clarify real outcomes, and set 30-day goals | Value record and success plan |
| Paid week 2 | Adoption check | Review volume, unanswered exceptions, staff follow-through, and fit | Two-week health score |
| Paid days 21–30 | First monthly value review | Reconcile calls through verified outcomes; collect friction and cancellation risk | 30-day scorecard |
| Monthly thereafter | Outcome and configuration review | Track value, update rules/capacity, and identify expansion or risk | Monthly customer health record |
| Storm/operating change | Event-driven configuration check | Validate capacity, hours, and escalation before conditions change | Approved change record |

Support responses should acknowledge the issue, state current ownership, distinguish customer versus Roof AI dependencies, and provide the next expected checkpoint. Do not promise a repair time until engineering or the service provider confirms it.

## Churn-risk signals and interventions

| Signal | Detection | Safe intervention | Owner/window |
|---|---|---|---|
| Setup inputs incomplete | Required fields still missing after Day 1 | Send/use the approved minimum-input checklist; name the single blocking item | Onboarding owner, same day |
| No working connection | Forwarding, notification, or calendar test fails | Troubleshoot the exact path; use request-and-confirm or remain off live traffic until fixed | Technical owner, before go-live |
| Operational first value absent | Test evidence missing by Day 4 | Run the controlled test suite and document output | Onboarding owner, same day |
| Misclassified callers | Repeated route errors or poor summaries | Review transcript/record, refine approved question or rule, retest | Workflow owner, within one business day |
| Staff handoffs not acted on | Qualified records remain unowned | Assign customer-side owner and notification escalation; do not count untouched handoffs as product wins | Customer success owner, weekly |
| Low live call volume | Few/no eligible misses in trial or month | Report insufficient volume honestly; assess coverage settings and fit, not fabricated ROI | Customer success owner, value review |
| High irrelevant volume | Spam, vendors, repeats, or customers dominate | Tighten eligibility and routing; deduplicate one need across attempts | Workflow owner, weekly |
| Capacity mismatch | Calendar closed, overbooked, or staff unavailable | Switch affected path to approved request-and-confirm; update capacity rules | Customer owner + Roof AI, immediately |
| Value disagreement | Customer expects every miss to become revenue | Reconcile definitions and records stage by stage | Account owner, before paid-readiness |
| Support friction | Repeated issue, unclear owner, or slow acknowledgment | Assign one incident owner and written next step; review root cause | Operations owner, same day |
| Billing surprise | Trial end or $299 charge misunderstood | Restate current published terms and escalation path; do not improvise a concession | Account owner, before Day 7 |
| Cancellation language | Pause, cancel, no value, too expensive, or replacement vendor mentioned | Record reason; perform a fit/value review; offer only approved resolution | Account owner, same business day |

Customer health is **red** for any safety/compliance issue, open critical defect, failed opt-out, wrong routing recipient, billing dispute, or direct cancellation request. It is **amber** for a missing owner, incomplete activation, declining usage, unresolved major defect, unworked handoffs, or unclear value. Green requires a healthy configuration, responsible customer owner, and current outcome review; raw call volume alone cannot create green status.

## Ethical risk reversal controlled by Roof AI

### Recommended concept: Guided Activation Commitment

Reduce implementation risk by promising only work Roof AI controls. Proposed internal promise:

> Roof AI will document the customer's approved call workflow, configure the agreed coverage, test the critical routes, deliver a go-live summary, and correct Roof AI-controlled configuration defects. Roofing opportunities, appointments, jobs, revenue, and incoming call volume are not guaranteed.

The commitment is fulfilled when the activation evidence is complete, regardless of whether a real missed call occurs during the seven-day trial.

### Boundaries

- Never guarantee response from a homeowner, qualified leads, estimates, signed work, collected revenue, or return on investment.
- Do not call every missed ring a lead or assign value without qualification.
- Exclude customer-controlled delays, unavailable carriers/calendars, inaccurate inputs, changed passwords, capacity changes, unsupported use cases, and third-party outages from any Roof AI-controlled commitment.
- A defect remediation must fix configuration or complete an agreed setup deliverable; it must not silently become a refund, free month, credit, trial extension, or pricing change.
- Any public wording, eligibility, remedy, billing effect, or legal term requires explicit commercial and legal approval before use.

### Safe remediation ladder

1. Correct the Roof AI-controlled configuration and rerun the failed test.
2. Provide a written root-cause and revised setup summary.
3. Add a time-bounded configuration review for the affected route.
4. Escalate any request for trial extension, credit, refund, or cancellation remedy for explicit approval.

This reverses setup uncertainty without transferring uncontrollable roofing-market risk to Roof AI.

## Weekly activation and retention scorecard

Report counts and rates together because the first-ten sample is small. Segment by acquisition source and onboarding cohort; never combine test events with real customer events.

### Funnel and revenue

| Metric | Definition | Initial target |
|---|---|---:|
| Qualified trials started | Trials passing the fit/readiness gate | Report count |
| Activated by Day 4 | All six activation conditions complete by end of Day 4 | At least 75% |
| Trial-to-first-paid | Successful first $299 charge ÷ qualified trials reaching decision | At least 67% |
| New paid accounts | First successful paid subscriptions | 2–3 per week toward 10/month |
| Gross new MRR | New paid accounts × $299 before deductions | $598–$897 per week |
| Failed/refunded first charges | First charges not retained, stated as count and dollars | Report; investigate every case |

### Activation health

| Metric | Definition | Initial target |
|---|---|---:|
| Core profile within 24 hours | Complete profile ÷ qualified trials | 90% |
| Connections within 48 hours | Working phone plus calendar/callback and notifications | 80% |
| Test suite passed within 72 hours | No open critical defect and required scenarios pass | 80% |
| Median time to activation | Trial start to all activation conditions complete | 4 days or less |
| Operational first value | Activated accounts with a recorded passing end-to-end test | 100% before paid-readiness |
| Open critical defects | Unresolved critical defects at weekly close | 0 |

### Adoption and value

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| Unique eligible missed calls | Distinct caller needs entering the approved workflow |
| Successful configured responses | Eligible calls receiving the configured first response |
| Two-way conversations | Unique eligible callers who engage |
| Qualified opportunities | Conversations meeting the customer's territory and job criteria |
| Approved handoffs | Callback, transfer, or notification reaching the correct destination |
| Confirmed estimates | Calendar-confirmed appointments; exclude preferred-time requests |
| Verified signed jobs | Customer-confirmed signed work attributable to one opportunity |
| Verified collected revenue | Customer-confirmed collected value under the agreed definition |
| Staff follow-through rate | Customer-owned actionable handoffs completed ÷ actionable handoffs |

### Retention and experience

| Metric | Definition | Initial target |
|---|---|---:|
| 30-day paid logo retention | Accounts still paid at Day 30 ÷ accounts first paid | At least 90% |
| Monthly logo churn | Paid accounts cancelled during month ÷ paid accounts at start | Below 5%; always show count |
| Green / amber / red accounts | Health status using the rules in this playbook | 100% assigned weekly |
| Support acknowledgment | Median and maximum time to acknowledge an issue | Establish baseline; approve an SLA before promising one |
| Unresolved issues aging | Open issues grouped by <1, 1–2, 3–7, and >7 days | Zero critical issues open at close |
| Cancellation reasons | Fit, setup, product, support, value, capacity, billing, competitor, or other | 100% coded |
| Reactivations | Previously cancelled accounts returning to paid | Report count |

### Weekly narrative

Attach five short answers to the scorecard:

1. Which account reached operational first value this week, and what evidence proves it?
2. Which account reached the most advanced verified market outcome?
3. Where did activation stall, and was the dependency controlled by Roof AI, the customer, or a third party?
4. Which repeated friction should become a product, documentation, or onboarding fix?
5. What single intervention is most likely to protect the next paid conversion or retained account?

## First-10 learning log

Maintain one row per account with:

- Account/cohort ID and original acquisition source
- Fit-gate date, trial start, target go-live, actual activation, and first-paid date
- Phone system, coverage model, calendar model, market, job mix, and call-volume band
- Completion timestamp for every milestone
- Test outcomes and defect severity
- First operational value and first market-value event
- Counts by funnel stage, without duplicate credit
- Support issues, owner, resolution, and aging
- Health status and risk reason
- Trial decision and cancellation/loss reason
- Customer-approved product feedback
- Any proof item, source record, and permission status

After customers 3, 5, and 10, conduct an internal cohort review. Change the playbook only when the evidence identifies a repeated issue; record the old rule, new rule, reason, effective date, and affected customers.

## Decision gates

Explicit approval is required before:

- contacting a trial or paid customer using this cadence;
- making a service-level, turnaround, or availability promise;
- changing the seven-day trial, $299/month price, billing timing, cancellation, refund, or credit terms;
- publishing the Guided Activation Commitment;
- requesting or publishing a testimonial, logo, call record, performance result, job, or revenue claim;
- using customer data in a case study, partner asset, advertisement, or public proof card; or
- changing consent, recording, texting, privacy, retention, or data-sharing practices.
