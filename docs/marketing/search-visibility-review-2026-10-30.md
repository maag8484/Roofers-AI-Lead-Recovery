# Search visibility review — October 30, 2026

## Result

The production site is technically healthy. The live sitemap contains 25 URLs, up from 24 on October 14 because the standalone missed-call demo is now included. All 25 returned HTTP 200 with no unexpected redirects, canonical conflicts, noindex directives, missing titles, missing descriptions or H1-count errors. Thirty unique internal destinations were checked and none was broken.

The scorecard, standalone demo, homepage callback anchor, signup route and partner route all resolved with HTTP 200. The homepage server response now includes one H1; the October 14 baseline recorded no H1 outside the client-rendered application. Current homepage metadata describes the free missed-call audit and 30-day pilot rather than the earlier $299 offer.

Crawlability is established. Comprehensive indexing, sitemap acceptance and Google rendering are not established by this crawl.

## Public search observations

These checks used public result pages on October 30. They are snapshots from one location and are not stable rankings or complete index inventories.

- Google: a direct public result request was rate-limited, so no exact Google position was recorded. Google URL Inspection and sitemap submission remain unverified.
- Bing RSS result sets: no Roof AI URL appeared for the brand query or the four buyer phrases in the returned sets.
- DuckDuckGo HTML result sets: Roof AI URLs appeared for the brand query, “missed call text back for roofers,” and “roofing answering service vs AI lead recovery.” No Roof AI URL appeared in the returned sets for “AI receptionist for roofing companies” or “after hours answering service for roofers.”

The public YouTube page for [Missed Call Recovery for Roofing Companies | Roof AI Demo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zu-E06YqS0E) was available. It displayed “No views” and one channel subscriber. Those public labels are not callback, customer or revenue evidence.

## Competitor evidence and highest-impact improvement

Local Call AI published a roofing case study on October 26 with original call-log denominators: 530 answered calls from one Texas roofer, including 131 calls with recorded outcomes and 11 AI-booked inspections. The vendor also published an owner-reported 42% booked-job increase, but supplied no signed-job denominator, collected revenue, refunds or independent audit: https://getlocalcall.com/case-studies/new-braunfels-roofing

The strongest improvement is to publish one verified Roof AI pilot case study after records and permission exist. It should report the full funnel—eligible missed calls, successful contacts, qualified opportunities, inspections held, signed jobs, cash collected, refunds and the measurement window—while separating call-log facts from customer testimony. Until then, keep demonstrations labeled as examples and do not add revenue claims. This improves buyer trust and gives search engines original evidence rather than another generic comparison page.
