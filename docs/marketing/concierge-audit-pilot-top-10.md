Prepared: September 4, 2026

## Pilot objective

Test whether a short, company-specific invitation to a Concierge Missed Revenue Audit can generate qualified conversations more effectively than sending a roofing company directly to a signup page.

The audit is a diagnostic offer, not a revenue promise. It should separate duplicate and irrelevant calls from possible new roofing opportunities, review the current response and handoff process, and identify what the company could measure. A missed call is not automatically a lost lead, and an estimate is not earned revenue.

## Selection design

The ten-company cohort is deliberately small enough to review every response and balanced across four launch markets:

- Charlotte: 3 companies
- Columbus: 2 companies
- DFW: 2 companies
- Jacksonville: 3 companies

Selection favors Tier 1 companies with a score of 8–9 and a public signal suggesting meaningful inbound demand, multiple call routes, storm or emergency intake, an office-hours handoff, or a measurable scheduling funnel. The cohort also mixes owner-led businesses, regional operators, and multi-market companies so results are not dependent on one company profile.

## First-touch operating standard

Every message below is intentionally sender-safe:

- Plain text; no link, attachment, image, tracking pixel, calendar link, or shortened URL.
- One truthful public observation and one low-friction question.
- No fake reply/forward prefix, false familiarity, manufactured urgency, or claim that Roof AI found lost revenue.
- No claim that every missed call is a lead, that the company has a problem, or that Roof AI will produce jobs or revenue.
- Replace `[First name]` only after the recipient and role are verified.
- Send from the approved Roof AI business sender with replies routed to the monitored inbox.
- Keep the company name and apostrophes exactly as verified before loading any campaign.

- Use weekdays only and the existing approved 9:30 a.m.–12:00 p.m. recipient-local/ET window as applicable; do not increase account volume to accommodate the pilot.
- Keep link tracking disabled. The first touch contains no link. Do not add a link to follow-ups without separate approval.

- Do not imply exclusivity, urgency, an existing relationship, detected missed calls, or access to the prospect’s private call data.
- After the second follow-up, close as `No response` and do not recycle into another sequence without a new reason and approval.

## Measurement and decision rule

Report counts and rates together. With a cohort of ten, results are directional and not statistically conclusive.

Primary stages:

1. Valid deliveries
2. Human replies
3. Positive replies
4. Qualified audit requests
5. Audits held
6. Demos held
7. Trials started
8. Trials activated
9. First paid accounts and new MRR
10. 30-day retained paid accounts

Diagnostic measures include bounce, wrong-person, opt-out, complaint, negative reply, no response, and time spent per audit. Never report “revenue recovered” unless a customer verifies the underlying opportunity and the agreed revenue stage. Keep estimated pipeline, signed contracts, completed work, and collected revenue separate.

After all ten companies reach a terminal first-touch disposition, review the cohort before expanding. Do not expand merely because delivery or open activity appears high. Expansion requires evidence of qualified replies or audits, sender health within agreed limits, manageable audit fulfillment, and explicit approval for the next recipients and copy.
