## Day 8 — LinkedIn Founder Post

**Post**

“We respond fast” can mean four different things inside a roofing company.

Did the homeowner receive an automatic acknowledgment?

Did a real two-way conversation begin?

Did someone confirm that the property and roofing need fit the company?

Did the caller receive a confirmed estimate or a clearly assigned callback?

Those are separate stages:

**Inquiry → acknowledgment → two-way response → qualification → confirmed next step**

If the team measures only the first automated message, it may believe the lead was handled when the homeowner is still waiting.

If it measures only booked estimates, it cannot see where otherwise legitimate opportunities stalled.

Start the clock at the first unique inbound event. Do not restart it every time the same homeowner calls again.

Then give each unresolved opportunity one accountable owner.

That is how response time becomes an operating process instead of a slogan.

## Day 9 — LinkedIn Company Post

**Post**

A missed-call text should do more than say, “Sorry we missed you.”

A practical roofing text-back workflow should:

1. Recognize one eligible missed call
2. Identify the roofing company and acknowledge the context
3. Ask one useful first question
4. Separate new opportunities from existing-customer requests
5. Qualify progressively instead of sending a long intake form
6. Offer an approved appointment, callback or handoff
7. Record the outcome

An example first question:

“Hi, this is [Roofing Company]. Sorry we missed your call. Are you contacting us about a repair, replacement, inspection or an existing project?”

The actual message should match the company’s services, brand and approved compliance language. Automated follow-up should be identified appropriately, respect opt-outs and never imply that a crew has been dispatched unless that is confirmed.

## Day 10 — YouTube Short 3

**Title:** The First Text After a Missed Roofing Call

**On-screen hook:** “Sorry we missed you” is not enough.

**Script (approximately 40 seconds)**

Your roofing company misses a call. What should the first text actually do?

First, identify the company and acknowledge the missed call.

Second, ask one short question that separates a new roofing need from an existing project or customer request.

For example: “Are you calling about a repair, replacement, inspection or an existing project?”

Do not send a ten-question form in the first message.

Do not promise an appointment, diagnosis or crew dispatch that has not been confirmed.

The goal is simple: begin a useful conversation and create a clear next step.

## Day 12 — LinkedIn Founder Post

**Post**

Roofing companies often evaluate marketing at the top of the funnel:

- Cost per click
- Cost per form
- Cost per call
- Number of leads

But the marketing investment does not stop when the phone rings.

If the call goes unanswered, the business still needs to make contact, identify whether the caller represents legitimate new work, confirm service-area fit and create a useful next step.

That is why two campaigns with the same cost per lead can produce very different business results.

One may generate calls the office can answer, qualify and schedule.

The other may generate demand during evenings, busy periods or storm surges—when no one owns the next step.

Before buying more volume, connect the current volume to:

**Unique inquiry → legitimate opportunity → qualified opportunity → booked estimate → signed job**

The biggest improvement may not be another lead source. It may be better handling of the demand you already created.

## Day 13 — LinkedIn Company Post

**Post**

Roofing lead qualification should help the office decide the next step—not force the homeowner through the entire sales process.

Start with four basics:

- Are you a new or existing customer?
- What is your name and best callback number?
- What is the property address?
- What are you calling about today?

For a potential new job, the next useful details may include:

- Residential, commercial or another property type
- Repair, replacement, inspection or storm-related need
- Reported active leaking or time-sensitive condition
- Service-area fit
- Property owner or authorized decision-maker
- Preferred appointment or callback timing

Ask only for information the company will actually use. If a question does not change routing, scheduling or the sales conversation, it may not belong in the first contact.
