## Day 15 — LinkedIn Founder Post

**Post**

A requested estimate time is not a booked roofing estimate.

That distinction sounds small until the office starts measuring conversion.

A homeowner might say Tuesday afternoon works. But if the calendar has not accepted the appointment, the estimator has not been assigned or the company still needs to confirm territory and project fit, the appointment is still a request.

A confirmed roofing estimate should establish:

- The property address
- The appointment type
- The agreed date and time
- Who will contact or visit the homeowner
- How to reschedule or cancel

This matters operationally and financially.

If requested times are counted as booked estimates, the funnel looks healthier than it is. If booked estimates are counted as revenue, it becomes even less reliable.

Keep the stages clean:

**Qualified opportunity → requested time → confirmed estimate → completed estimate → signed job**

## Day 16 — LinkedIn Company Post

**Post**

Direct booking can shorten the path from roofing inquiry to estimate—but only when the calendar reflects reality.

Before allowing an inbound workflow to schedule estimates, define:

- Exact service territories
- Appointment types and durations
- Drive-time and preparation buffers
- Which estimator handles each job type
- Minimum notice and same-day limits
- Daily and weekly capacity
- Weather, meeting and field-work blocks
- Rescheduling and cancellation rules

A residential replacement estimate, repair evaluation, emergency tarp request and commercial inspection may require different people and different appointment windows.

If availability or territory rules cannot stay current, use request-and-confirm instead of presenting the appointment as booked.

The right scheduling model is the one the roofing company can honor consistently.

## Day 17 — YouTube Short 4

**Title:** A Requested Time Is Not a Booked Roofing Estimate

**On-screen hook:** “Tuesday works” does not mean the job is on the calendar.

**Script (approximately 35 seconds)**

A homeowner says Tuesday afternoon works for a roofing estimate.

Is the appointment booked?

Only if the calendar accepts it and the confirmation states the property, appointment type, date, time and what happens next.

If the service area still needs review, the estimator is unavailable or the time is only a preference, call it an appointment request.

Why does the wording matter?

Because requested times, confirmed estimates, completed estimates and signed jobs are different stages.

Clean measurement starts with honest definitions.

## Day 20 — LinkedIn Company Post

**Post**

A low roofing cost per lead can hide an expensive conversion problem.

Use a separate cost at each stage:

**Cost per raw inquiry**
Channel cost ÷ unique inquiries

**Cost per legitimate opportunity**
Channel cost ÷ relevant potential new roofing jobs

**Cost per qualified opportunity**
Channel cost ÷ opportunities fitting service and territory criteria

**Cost per booked estimate**
Agreed marketing and handling cost ÷ confirmed estimate appointments

Why can the numbers diverge?

- Duplicate calls inflate inquiry volume
- Spam, vendors or customers are counted as leads
- Properties fall outside the service area
- The team cannot make contact
- Qualified callers do not receive a usable appointment path
- Requested times are counted as confirmed

Define the numerator and denominator, then use the same definitions month after month.
