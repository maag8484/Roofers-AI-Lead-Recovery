When a traditional answering service makes sense

It may be the better fit when every inbound call should reach a person, including billing questions, supplier calls, existing-project updates and conversations requiring broad judgment.

  • Are evenings, weekends and storm surges included?
  • Are calls billed per minute, per call or through an allowance?
  • Can agents schedule directly on the roofing company’s calendar?
  • How are new leads separated from customers and solicitations?
  • What happens when volume exceeds the plan?
  • Can the roofer review outcomes, not only message transcripts?

When AI lead recovery makes sense

Missed-call recovery can fit a team that already answers many calls but cannot answer all of them—when staff are on another line, estimators are in the field, the office is closed or storm activity creates a surge.

The purpose is not to generate another lead. It is to give an inbound caller the company already attracted a clear next step.

  1. Recognize an unanswered call.
  2. Acknowledge it promptly using the configured channel.
  3. Identify a new prospect, existing customer or other caller.
  4. Collect the property, requested service, service area and urgency.
  5. Offer an approved appointment or handoff.
  6. Record what happened so the roofer can measure it.

The two models are not mutually exclusive. A roofer may use live answering as the first layer and recovery when a call still slips through.

How to compare the real cost

Do not compare only advertised monthly prices. Request an itemized explanation of the base subscription, usage, after-hours coverage, overages, setup, integrations, calendar booking, transfers, reporting and staff review time.

Two useful operating measures

Cost per qualified opportunity = service cost ÷ qualified opportunities attributable to the service

Cost per booked estimate = service cost ÷ confirmed estimates booked through the service

A booked estimate is not a completed sale, and estimated pipeline is not earned revenue.

A worked example — replace every number with your own

Take one recent 30-day period from your own call log and phone bill. Suppose the log shows 20 missed calls that were not spam, vendors or existing-project callers; 8 got a documented next step; 3 booked inspections; the answering service cost $400; and a recovery layer would cost $199 at the founding rate. These are illustrative placeholders, not benchmarks or promises.

  • Cost per qualified opportunity (answering service alone): $400 ÷ 8 = $50.
  • Cost per booked estimate (answering service alone): $400 ÷ 3 = about $133.

The question is what the second layer would need to add to justify $199. If recovery hypothetically turned 4 more of the 12 unhandled callers into a next step and 1 more into a booked inspection, combined cost per booked estimate would be ($400 + $199) ÷ 4 = about $150. Whether that is worthwhile depends on your own close rate and job value. Run the same arithmetic on your own records; do not decide from this example's numbers.

Using both: answering service as the first layer, recovery as the safety net

A roofing company does not have to choose one or the other. The common setup is two layers:

  1. The answering service answers first during covered hours, using the company's script and passing the message on.
  2. Recovery catches what still slips through—calls the service could not reach, calls outside covered hours, and calls that went to voicemail. Recovery follows up using approved wording and qualification questions.

One owner per caller: once a caller has a named next step, the other layer stops. One record per caller: consolidate message, address, requested service and outcome. One agreed handoff: decide in advance what escalates tonight and what waits until morning.

If the answering service already owns every next step, the remaining gap may be small—run the free missed-call audit before adding another layer.

Before you cancel your answering service

If replacing rather than adding a layer, check these items before giving notice:

  1. Contract and notice: confirm the notice period and any early-termination charge.
  2. The number: confirm the business number stays yours, remove forwarding cleanly, and test it before switching off the old service.
  3. Message history: export recent messages and open callbacks before access ends.
  4. Overlap week: run the new setup alongside the old one for a week if the contract allows it, then compare what each actually caught. See the call-overflow plan.

If any item cannot be confirmed, keep the answering service and add recovery as the second layer instead.

Frequently asked questions

Can I keep my answering service and add AI lead recovery?

Yes. The answering service handles covered calls; recovery catches calls that slip through. Once a caller has a named next step, the other layer stops.

What does AI lead recovery do that my answering service does not?

An answering service takes a message and passes it on; recovery follows up on the missed call, asks roofing qualification questions and offers an approved appointment or handoff, then records the outcome.

How do I know which one is worth the money?

Compare cost per booked estimate using your own call log and confirmed estimates from the same period. A booked estimate is not a completed sale, and estimated pipeline is not earned revenue.
