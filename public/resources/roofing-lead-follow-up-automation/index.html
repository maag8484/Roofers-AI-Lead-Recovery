Automation framework

Automate Roofing Lead Follow-Up Without Losing Human Ownership

Automation should shorten the time to a useful next step, not create a high-volume sequence that ignores replies, declines or context.

The most useful automation starts after an approved inbound event: a missed call, a requested callback or a submitted form. It should stop as soon as the caller responds or a person takes ownership.

Define the trigger precisely

Use a verified event such as an eligible unanswered call. Exclude spam, repeat attempts already owned by staff, existing-customer service calls that need another route and any caller who has opted out.

Use one useful first response

Identify the roofing company, explain why the message is being sent and ask one easy operational question. Do not claim the caller lost money, qualify for insurance or has an appointment.

Set stop conditions before launch

  • Stop on any human reply
  • Stop on decline or opt-out
  • Stop when staff assumes ownership
  • Stop when the need is unsupported or outside the service area
  • Escalate urgent or ambiguous situations according to company rules

Keep the human decision point

Automation can collect information and create the next-step request. A human still owns exceptions, estimates, insurance guidance, complaints and any promise that commits the roofing company.

Measure the complete funnel

Count eligible missed calls, responses, completed qualifications, qualified opportunities, callback completion, requested and held appointments, signed jobs and paid invoices. Report each conversion with its denominator.
