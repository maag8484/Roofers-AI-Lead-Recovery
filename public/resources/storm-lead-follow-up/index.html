Storm Lead Follow-Up: A Practical Workflow for Roofing Companies

Storm demand can arrive faster than an office can answer. Use one intake and triage process without diagnosing damage or promising dispatch the team has not confirmed.

A storm queue can mix active leaks, inspection requests, existing-customer concerns, insurance questions, solicitors and properties outside the service area. Consistency makes that demand easier to review.

Build the operating foundation first with the after-hours answering guide for roofing companies.

Prepare before the storm

  • Publish current service areas and job types.
  • Set capacity and appointment rules for storm mode.
  • Choose who receives urgent handoffs.
  • Write approved safety and escalation language.
  • Define customer, prospect and out-of-area routes.
  • Test what happens when calendars or transfers are unavailable.

A five-stage surge workflow

  1. Acknowledge. Identify the roofing company and confirm the inquiry was received.
  2. Classify. New prospect, current customer, vendor or other caller.
  3. Capture. Property, contact, reported need, active water entry and relevant timing.
  4. Check fit. Service area, work type and current capacity.
  5. Route. Approved appointment, callback request, existing-customer notification or human review.

The follow-up cadence

Adapt this starting pattern to your actual capacity; it is not a guaranteed result. Qualify at first contact and give an achievable next step.

  1. First contact: acknowledge, classify and capture the inquiry.
  2. Same day: attempt again at a different time if no connection.
  3. Day 1 after inspection: send promised photos and written notes.
  4. Day 3: check on the estimate without unverified insurance advice.
  5. Week 2: one decision check-in; monthly only if requested.
  6. Stop on booking, decline, opt-out, out-of-area or disqualification; record the reason.

Four follow-up messages you can adapt

Starting templates, not guaranteed replies or jobs.

Same-day second attempt

“Hi, this is [Name] with [Company]. We received your storm-damage call for [address]. Would [approved window A] or [approved window B] work for an inspection? Reply STOP to opt out.”

Day 1 after inspection

“Hi, this is [Name] with [Company]. Here are the promised inspection photos and notes for [address]: [link]. Please reply with any questions. Reply STOP to opt out.”

Insurance-waiting lane

“Hi, this is [Name] with [Company]. We have paused sales follow-up while you wait for your adjuster. Can you share the meeting date when known? Reply STOP to opt out.”

Week 2 no-reply check-in

“Hi, this is [Name] with [Company]. Would you like us to keep the estimate file for [address] open or close it? Reply STOP to opt out.”

Use consent-aware text-back rules. Do not present an automated text as a live employee or claim coverage, dispatch or arrival without confirmation.

Prioritize without overpromising

Priority can reflect what the caller reports and the company’s operating rules. It should not be presented as a technical diagnosis. Do not state that insurance will cover damage, a crew is dispatched, or an arrival time is guaranteed unless those facts are confirmed.

Safety boundary

Possible structural instability, fallen electrical lines or other immediate threats should follow approved emergency language and be directed to the appropriate emergency service. A lead workflow is not an emergency-response service.

Protect the schedule

When storm demand exceeds estimator capacity, switch from open booking to controlled windows or request-and-confirm. Maintain buffers for travel, weather and job type. The roofing appointment scheduling guide explains the choice. For each lead, record the next touch and final result in the follow-up automation workflow.

Give the office a usable queue

Each record should show the caller, property, contact method, new-versus-existing status, reported issue, service-area fit, requested next step, assigned owner and current disposition. Add claim number and adjuster meeting date for insurance-waiting cases, plus last-touch date, next-touch date and closing reason (booked / declined / asked to stop / out of area / unqualified). Repeated calls, texts and forms for the same property stay on one record. Pause sales follow-ups while awaiting an adjuster; keep only an informational check-in. Use one CRM record per property so each touch and outcome stays together.

Review outcomes after the surge using the missed-call scorecard

  • Unique storm-related inquiries
  • Two-way responses
  • Qualified in-area opportunities
  • Appointments requested, booked and completed
  • Existing-customer issues routed
  • Unqualified or out-of-area contacts
  • Jobs verified later in company records
  • Inquiries with a recorded second touch
  • Records closed with a reason

Know the possible value inside the queue

Use your own opportunity rate, close rate and job value rather than a storm-season industry guess.
