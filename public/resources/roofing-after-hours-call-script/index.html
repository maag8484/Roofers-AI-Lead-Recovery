A Roofing After-Hours Call Script That Creates a Clear Next Step

The script should acknowledge the caller, collect only useful information and set an expectation the roofing company can actually support.

Updated: 2026-11-23

A good after-hours response does not pretend the office is open. It clearly identifies the roofing company’s process and moves an eligible inquiry toward a callback or approved scheduling route.

This is a voice script, not a text-back script: the words the answering voice uses on a live after-hours call. It names the company and says the office is closed, asks one question that sorts the call (new project, existing job, or something else), collects only the details the team needs, and offers only the callback or scheduling action the company approved. It never pretends the office is open or promises a crew, a price, or an arrival time. See after-hours answering coverage for the workflow the script runs inside, or the missed-call text-back guide for the text sent after a missed call.

Recommended structure

  1. Identify: State the roofing company and that the call is being handled after hours.
  2. Ask: “Are you calling about a new roofing project, an existing job or something else?”
  3. Qualify: Collect the property city, roofing need and urgency using the short qualification questions.
  4. Set the next step: Offer only the callback or scheduling action the company approved.
  5. Confirm: Repeat the name, phone number and next step once.

Copy-ready voice templates by caller lane

Adapt each template to the company’s actual service area, staffing and approved next step. Replace bracketed fields before use. An automated answer must identify itself as automated; a live person uses their own name. Never present an automated answer as a live employee.

Active leak — template

“Thanks for calling [Company]. [Approved truthful speaker identification]. Our office is closed. Is water entering right now, and what is the property address? I can record this for [approved priority callback or first-light review]. If water is near wiring, a ceiling is sagging, or anyone is in danger, contact emergency services first and stay away from the hazard. This is not a crew dispatch or arrival promise.”

Routine estimate / replacement — template

“Thanks for calling [Company]. [Approved truthful speaker identification]. Our office is closed. What is happening with the roof, and what city is the property in? I can take a request for [approved next business-hours callback window or booking request]. This is a request, not a confirmed appointment.”

Existing customer — template

“Thanks for calling [Company]. [Approved truthful speaker identification]. Our office is closed. What is the job address, and can you describe the issue in one line? I will send the message to [team member who owns the job] under our approved handoff process. I cannot diagnose the issue or promise a repair or arrival time.”

Storm surge — template

“Thanks for calling [Company]. [Approved truthful speaker identification]. Our office is closed and call volume is high after the storm. What is the property address, and what can you see safely from the ground? Our review order is active leaks first, visible damage second, general checks third. Your request will be recorded for [named owner and approved response window]. This does not assign a numbered queue position or an inspection date.”

Define the capacity limit in the call overflow plan and continue the queue in the storm lead follow-up workflow. Only report a queue category after the caller’s details establish it; never invent a position or date.

Route safety concerns first

If a caller reports water near wiring, a sagging ceiling or anyone in danger, route them to the appropriate emergency services first. Do not diagnose the situation, give temporary-repair instructions or promise a crew. Never tell a caller to climb onto a roof or inspect a hazardous area. A photo from the ground or inside may be requested only when it is safe. Dangerous or life-safety situations are not normal roofing appointments.

Holiday and closed-day wording

State the actual holiday closure and reopening day: “The [Company] office is closed for [holiday] and reopens [actual day and time]. [Approved truthful speaker identification]. What is the issue and property location, is there active water or visible damage, and what callback window works after reopening?” Offer only the company’s approved response window; do not imply a crew is coming. Use the same safety routing for callers in danger. See winter call handling for winter-night wording.

What the script should never promise

  • Insurance approval or coverage
  • Pricing or a job estimate
  • Emergency arrival times
  • A confirmed appointment without a verified calendar action
  • Service outside the approved area

Frequently asked questions

Is this script for a live person or an automated answer?

Either. The words follow the same structure, but an automated answer must identify itself honestly and never present itself as a live employee. The script never pretends the office is open and offers only the callback or scheduling action the company approved.

What should an after-hours script never promise?

Insurance approval or coverage, a price or job estimate, emergency arrival times, a confirmed appointment without a verified calendar action, or service outside the approved area.

How do we test the script before it goes live?

Place an authorized test call after hours and check the conversation, the captured details, the callback or booking request, the owner notification and the audit trail. If any check fails, any promise from the never-promise list is spoken, or a safety situation is routed as a routine callback, correct the workflow before real callers go through it.
