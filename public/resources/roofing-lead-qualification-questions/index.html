Roofing Lead Qualification Questions That Help Prioritize Follow-Up

Collect enough information to decide the next step—without asking the homeowner to complete the whole sales process before speaking with the roofer.

The right questions depend on the company’s services and sales process. Use this framework to build separate routes for new prospects, existing customers and urgent exceptions.

For the larger operating context, read the 2026 guide to AI receptionists for roofing companies.

Start with identity and intent

  1. Are you a new customer or an existing customer?
  2. What is your name and best callback number?
  3. What is the property address?
  4. What are you calling about today?

These questions determine the route before the workflow asks for project details. A warranty or scheduling concern should not be counted as a new lead.

For a new roofing opportunity

  • Is the property residential, commercial or another type?
  • Are you looking for a repair, replacement, inspection or storm-damage assessment?
  • Is there active leaking or another time-sensitive condition?
  • Is the property within the company’s service area?
  • Are you the property owner or authorized decision-maker?
  • What is the preferred next step and best availability?

Ask only questions the company will use. If a detail does not affect routing, scheduling or the sales conversation, it may not belong in first contact.

If the property is commercial, four questions replace the residential ones above: who is the facility contact and who makes the roofing decision; is this a budget-cycle item or an urgent repair; is there a pre-qualification or vendor-approval process before a proposal; and what is the deadline for the proposal. A commercial lead with no named decision-maker and no deadline is a “qualified, staff review” outcome, not a booked inspection.

Three questions the first call is missing

The questions above decide the route. These three decide what the job is — ask them only when the answer changes routing, scheduling, or the sales conversation.

How old is the roof, and what is it made of?

“Do you know roughly how old the roof is, and is it shingle, metal, tile, or something else?” A 22-year-old asphalt roof and a 4-year-old metal roof are different conversations; the answer tells the estimator what to prepare for, not what to charge. If the homeowner does not know, record “unknown” and move on — do not guess an age or a material from the answers.

Is an insurance claim involved, and where does it stand?

“Have you filed a claim for this, and has an adjuster been out yet?” Record only what the homeowner reports: claim filed or not, adjuster visit scheduled or done, carrier name if volunteered. Do not interpret the claim, do not say whether it will be approved, and do not promise coverage. Coverage is for the estimator and the adjuster — the intake question only records where the claim stands so the right person sees it first.

When do you want this done, and what have you set aside?

“When are you looking to get this handled — this week, this month, or further out?” and, only if the company uses it to route, “Have you set aside a rough budget for the work?” A homeowner who says next spring is not this week’s site visit. A budget answer is a range the homeowner volunteers, never a requirement to book, and never a basis for quoting a price on this call. If they will not answer, record “not shared” and keep the booking.

When the calls surge: four questions only

After a storm, the full route above is too long — the caller will be on the next roofer’s line before question six. For surge hours, use four questions and stop:

  1. What is your name and best callback number?
  2. What is the property address?
  3. Is water coming in right now, or do you need an estimate?
  4. Is the property inside our service area?

Then give the next step: an inspection time, or a place on the callback list in the order calls arrived. Everything else — roof age, claim status, budget, timeline — is collected at the callback or the inspection, not on the surge call. For an existing customer

  • Which property or project is this about?
  • Is the request about scheduling, billing, active work, service or warranty?
  • Who has been the company contact?
  • What happened, and when?
  • What is the requested callback timing?

Questions that require careful limits

An intake system can collect what the homeowner reports, but it should not diagnose damage, promise insurance coverage, state that a claim will be approved, guarantee financing, or promise crew arrival without confirmation.

Urgency is not the same as emergency dispatch

Ask whether water is actively entering or whether there is a possible safety concern, then follow company-approved escalation language. Immediate threats to personal safety should be directed to the appropriate emergency service.

When the caller will not answer a question

Some callers will not give an address, a budget, or a timeline on a first call. That is not a failed qualification — it is a partial record. Do not block the booking on a refused question, and do not guess the missing answer from the answers you have. Record the question as “not shared”, route on what you do know (identity, intent, service area, urgency), and let the estimator or the callback collect the rest. A record that says “not shared” in plain sight is more useful than one that looks complete because somebody filled in a guess.

Turn answers into clear outcomes

  • Qualified and schedulable: fits services, territory and available appointment rules.
  • Qualified, staff review: may fit but requires judgment or a special appointment.
  • Existing-customer handoff: routed to the service or project path.
  • Out of area or service mismatch: recorded accurately, not counted as a sales loss.
  • Needs a person: upset caller, unusual request or uncertain conversation.

Next, map those outcomes into a real roofing estimate scheduling workflow. Make the callback timing part of the outcome so the next step has an owner and a clock.

What the estimator receives: a sample handoff record

The five outcomes above are only useful if the person doing the next step can read them at a glance. One record, one outcome, one next step:

Illustrative sample — not a real customer or a real call.

  • Caller: [name], [callback number]
  • Property: [address], residential, in service area
  • Job: storm-damage inspection; roof age and material unknown
  • Claim: filed, adjuster visit scheduled [date]
  • Timeline: this week; budget not shared
  • Outcome: qualified and schedulable
  • Next step: inspection [date/time window], estimator [name]
  • Notes: active leak reported — contractor-approved escalation language used; no coverage, price, or arrival promise made

If any line above is missing, the record is still usable as long as the missing line says “unknown” or “not shared” instead of being left blank. See the CRM workflow guide for how this record is stored and handed off without being re-typed.

Test with realistic calls

Run a replacement inquiry, active leak, out-of-area request, existing warranty customer, commercial project and direct request for a human before launch.

Each test call passes only if three things are true: the call reached the right outcome from “Turn answers into clear outcomes” above, no promise was made about damage, coverage, price, or crew arrival, and the record shows the next step with a named owner. A test that books the wrong route, makes a promise, or leaves the record without an owner has failed — fix the route, not the caller.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions should I ask on the first call?

Only the questions the company will use. Identity and intent first, then three to five routing questions — owner, service area, job type, urgency, and the next step. Everything else belongs at the callback or the inspection, not on the first call.

Should I ask about budget on the first call?

Ask what the homeowner has set aside only if the company uses the answer to route the job. It is a range the homeowner volunteers, never a requirement to book, and never a basis for quoting a price on this call. If they will not answer, record “not shared” and keep the booking.

What if the homeowner has already filed an insurance claim?

Record the claim status — filed or not, adjuster visit scheduled or done, carrier name if volunteered — and pass it to the estimator. Do not interpret the claim, promise coverage, or act as an adjuster. Coverage is decided by the estimator and the adjuster, not on the intake call.
