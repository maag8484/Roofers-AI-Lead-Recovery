How Fast Should a Roofer Call Back?

“Respond fast” is not a complete process. Define the clock, the owner and the next step so the team can see where an inbound opportunity stalls.

Response time matters because an unanswered homeowner is still making a decision. Fast contact cannot guarantee a job, but it can reduce uncertainty and keep a roofing inquiry from sitting unowned.

The short answer

Inside five minutes for first contact if you can; inside the hour at the latest. The first reply should do more than confirm receipt — ask one sorting question and offer an inspection time.

The studies behind the five-minute figure examined web leads generally, not roofing companies specifically, and measured contact or qualification rather than signed jobs. Treat those findings as direction, not a promise. Measure your own response times instead of adopting someone else's benchmark.

What the widely cited studies say — and what they don't prove

The MIT/Oldroyd Lead Response Management study reported that leads contacted within five minutes were roughly 21 times more likely to qualify than leads contacted at 30 minutes; it also found that the odds of reaching a lead fell sharply after 30 minutes.

Harvard Business Review's 2011 analysis of 1.25 million web leads reported that firms reaching a lead within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify it as firms that waited an additional hour.

Velocify's research reported that contact within the first minute produced the highest conversion of the time windows it examined.

Roofing Contractor's 2026 homeowner survey, as reported by Renova Reach, found that 66% of homeowners gather three quotes and that 14% of contractors said they reply within five minutes. That gap is a reason to examine your process, not a guarantee of an outcome.

These studies were not conducted on roofing companies, and none tracked signed roofing jobs. Their figures describe general web-lead contact or qualification findings, not Roof AI results or a promise that a faster reply will win a particular job.

Set your own target from your own baseline

Take one recent seven-day period — or 30 days if your call volume is low — and record first-contact, two-way response, qualification and booking times. Split the results by lead source and time window, including business hours, after hours and storm periods.

Your next target should improve on your own previous period, not simply copy a borrowed benchmark. For example, if your median first contact last week was four hours, a 30-minute median next week is a meaningful improvement for your process; five minutes is direction, not the pass mark.

Name one owner for each window: business hours, after hours and storm surge. Any lead with no documented next step after 24 hours is an unresolved exception to review weekly.

Start the clock at the real inbound event

Choose a consistent starting point: the moment the first unique call is missed, a web form arrives, or another approved lead source creates an inquiry. Do not reset the clock when the same homeowner calls twice.

Build one ownership rule

Every inbound opportunity needs an accountable next owner. Automation may acknowledge and qualify the caller, but the company must decide who receives urgent issues, exceptions, estimate requests and conversations that require a person. Document the handoff in a roofing CRM missed-call workflow.

Measure by source and time window

  • Business-hours versus after-hours inquiries — use the after-hours call script and after-hours answering guide to define the route
  • Calls versus forms and other channels
  • Paid, organic, referral and repeat-customer sources
  • Normal weeks versus storm-related surges — use the storm lead follow-up guide to review surge handling
  • Initial response, two-way contact and appointment time

A single blended average can hide the exact shift, source or route causing the delay. Review the distribution and the unresolved exceptions, not only one headline number.

A practical improvement loop

  1. Document the current path from inquiry to owner.
  2. Find periods when the queue has no coverage.
  3. Use missed-call text-back or another approved response for eligible gaps.
  4. Separate acknowledgment from qualification and booking.
  5. Review unresolved conversations weekly with a documented follow-up automation and owner.
  6. Update routing and scheduling rules when real calls expose a gap.

Frequently asked questions

How fast should a roofer call back a new lead?

Inside five minutes if you can, and inside the hour at the latest. The first reply should ask one sorting question and offer an inspection time.

Does responding in five minutes guarantee the job?

No. The cited studies measured contact and qualification, not signed contracts, and were not run on roofing companies. Speed can help start the first conversation; the estimate and the close still decide the job.

What should I measure if I don't track response time yet?

Start with first-contact time only, split between business hours and after hours, for one recent week. Add qualification and booking times once the first number is stable.
