Funnel economics

Cost per Roofing Lead vs Cost per Booked Estimate

Cost per lead is what you paid to make the phone ring. Cost per booked estimate is what you paid for a confirmed inspection on the calendar: cost per lead divided by the share of leads that book. A cheap lead that never books can cost more per estimate than an expensive lead that does. An unanswered lead remains in the cost pool even if you never make contact.

Updated: 2026-11-22

Use the same cost pool and the same deduplicated lead cohort for both figures. Cost per booked estimate = total agreed cost ÷ confirmed estimates = cost per lead ÷ overall booking rate. Overall booking rate means confirmed estimates divided by all leads in that cohort, not just the people you reached. With zero confirmed estimates, cost per booked estimate is undefined; report zero bookings rather than dividing by zero.

These rows are accounting views, not a claim that every inquiry is contacted in the displayed order. Count reached opportunities from a real two-way exchange, not a delivered message. The scorecard worksheet gives the counting method, and the lead response-time guide explains what contact means. Use the same cost pool when comparing stage costs; label a fully loaded cost separately if it includes handling or recovery fees.

Why the numbers diverge

  • Duplicate calls inflate raw inquiry volume.
  • Spam, vendors and customers are counted as leads.
  • Properties fall outside the service area.
  • The company cannot make contact.
  • Qualified callers do not receive a usable appointment path.
  • Requested times are counted as booked before confirmation.

Include the right costs

For a channel-level view, include campaign and platform spend. For a full booked-estimate view, also decide whether to include call handling, lead services, recovery tools and attributable sales labor. State the definition so month-to-month comparisons remain consistent.

Example method using your data

  1. Choose one source and reporting period.
  2. Deduplicate inquiries tied to the same roofing need.
  3. Record which unique opportunities were actually reached, then classify legitimate and qualified opportunities.
  4. Confirm which appointments reached the calendar.
  5. Divide the agreed cost pool by each stage.
  6. Compare stages to locate the largest drop.

Do not use an invented industry benchmark when your own service mix, territory and capacity determine the real economics.

Run it once with your own numbers

Illustration only — hypothetical inputs, not a benchmark, a customer result or a recovery promise. Replace every figure with your own records. Assume a $5,000 channel cost for 100 unique leads: $50 per lead. For this arithmetic only, all 100 are legitimate; 80% of contacted leads qualify and 50% of qualified leads reach a confirmed inspection. Add downstream context

Cost per booked estimate is the agreed cost pool divided by confirmed calendar inspections. Cost per booked job / signed job applies the actual estimate-to-signed-job close rate after that stage: with the same cohort and cost pool, cost per signed job = cost per booked estimate ÷ close rate. A signed job is not collected revenue.

Booked estimates still need completion and close-rate data. A channel with a higher booked-estimate cost may produce better-fit projects or larger jobs. Keep signed contract value, gross profit and collected revenue distinct.

Frequently asked questions

Is cost per lead or cost per booked estimate the better number?

Cost per booked estimate is the more useful number for evaluating confirmed calendar outcomes. Cost per lead only prices the top of the funnel. Keep the cost definition and lead cohort consistent, and use signed-job, profit and collected-revenue data for downstream decisions.

How do I calculate cost per booked estimate?

Divide cost per lead by the overall booking rate, using your own deduplicated contacted, qualified and confirmed-booking counts. Overall booking rate is confirmed estimates divided by all leads in the same cohort. Equivalently, divide the agreed total cost pool by confirmed estimates. With zero bookings, report zero bookings; the cost per booked estimate is undefined.

Why is my cost per booked estimate higher than my cost per lead suggests?

Because unanswered, unqualified and unconfirmed leads remain in the cost pool. Count them from your own call log, distinguish a delivered message from a conversation and a requested time from a confirmed booking, and state whether handling and recovery costs are included.
